By: Eoghan Olkeriil Ngirudelsang

MELEKEOK, Palau (Oct. 30, 2026) — Ebil-Reklai Mitsko Walter, the head of Melekeok’s council of female chiefs and a leading traditional figure in Palau’s eastern region, died Wednesday, Oct. 21, after serving in the cultural role since March 2024.

Walter held the title of Ebil-Reklai, the female counterpart to the High Chief Reklai, and served as the head matriarch of Desbedall, Palau’s eastern region.

She assumed the title following the death of Ebil-Reklai Gracia Demei Yalap, who died July 1, 2023, after holding the title since 2009.

Walter’s death has prompted a traditional mourning period in Melekeok, as the community and the nation mourn her passing and her service to Palau.

By: Summer Kennard

KOROR, Palau — “I’m proud of being able to make it to top 3, but I know I’m more capable compared to the performance I had,” Palauan runner Kraven Ngirchomlei said after finishing second in the boys’ 5-kilometer race at the Asia Pacific Invitational (API) Cross Country Championships, marking the first time Palau has hosted the regional competition.

Ngirchomlei, a senior at Palau High School, clocked 18 minutes, 29.56 seconds in the race held Oct. 23-24. Garrett Welge of Belau All Island finished third in 18:56.54, while Jan Tibigar of the JFK Islanders from Guam won in 18:15.01.

The two-day championships brought together 124 runners for boys’ and girls’ 5-kilometer races and a 2-by-1-mile relay. Visiting teams included the JFK Islanders and Guam Adventist Academy, both from Guam.

Ngirchomlei said he was pleased to finish among the top three but believed he could have achieved a better time and result.

He said he started the race conservatively, following a pacing strategy that caused him to hold back too much energy. That approach left him unable to close the gap with the runner ahead of him.

“I’m glad it happened now that I’ve learned from it, so I wouldn’t make the mistake again,” he said.

Having competed in other locations, Ngirchomlei said the API race was less challenging than some of his previous competitions. He also appreciated the opportunity to compete against runners whose abilities were closer to his own.

As he prepares to graduate from high school, Ngirchomlei said he plans to continue running and hopes to represent Palau on the national team.

“I definitely see my future self competing in the sport of running and plan on continuing to race for our national team,” he said.

Welge, also a high school senior, said he gave the race his best effort but made mistakes during the competition. He was particularly struck by the changing pace and positions of runners throughout the race, as competitors pushed to catch those ahead of them.

“The biggest thing that I was surprised at as a runner was how dynamic the overall flow of the race was,” Welge said. “There were people dropping off and moving up, constantly trying to reach the next guy. It was fun.”

Welge said the experience reinforced two lessons: There will always be faster runners to learn from, and athletes must move forward after each competition, regardless of the result.

“I learned two lessons from this race, one, there’s always someone faster than you out there and two, after the race you gotta move on,” he said. “It doesn’t matter if it was good or bad, reflect then move on.”

Welge hopes to qualify for collegiate competition as he approaches graduation, continuing to develop his running skills and compete at a higher level.

“I’m going to try my absolute best to try and be able to qualify and compete in college,” he said.

He added that he plans to keep running even if he does not pursue the sport competitively at the collegiate level.

Palauan paddlers compete in modified knockout format as organizers prioritize safety

By: Summer Kennard

AGANA, Guam  –  “ I was happy to hear that the races had been moved earlier because it meant we would still have the opportunity to compete,” said Kedals Reklai of Palau’s AMF outrigger canoe club, after organizers moved Guam’s Micro Cup Outrigger Canoe Race ahead of schedule to prepare for Typhoon Champi.

Three Palauan outrigger canoe clubs — AMF, Belau Kanu Club (BKC) and Iull — participated in the competition, which was originally scheduled for Oct. 16-18 but was moved to Oct. 14-15 as the approaching typhoon threatened the event.

Organizers canceled the planned weekend program but held an official race on Oct. 15 to give visiting teams an opportunity to compete before returning home.

Guam organizer Melanie said approximately 10 to 12 teams were represented across the one-man, four-man and six-man races.

The revised competition used a knockout format instead of timed results. Paddlers raced in sprints along a 250-meter course, with approximately 40 seconds between one finish and the next start. The last paddler or crew to finish each sprint was eliminated, and the rounds continued until one paddler or crew remained.

Melanie said the schedule change was made to protect athletes, volunteers and the wider community. Organizers met directly with coaches and participating teams to communicate the revised schedule.

Despite the disruption, Melanie described the event as successful, saying paddlers enjoyed the venue and maintained a strong competitive spirit.

For Reklai, who made her debut in the open V1 women’s category, the schedule change meant she could still compete despite the possibility that the event would be canceled.

“There was a possibility that the event would be canceled due to Typhoon Champi, so I was grateful that the organizers found a way for the competition to continue,” Reklai said.

She said her teammates stayed positive and focused as they adjusted to the changes.

“We understood that we had to adjust quickly, support one another, and make the most of the opportunity we were given,” she said.

Reklai said competitions such as the Micro Cup allow paddlers to evaluate their performance, identify areas for improvement and learn from other athletes. The experience also helps develop paddling skills, confidence and teamwork, she said.

Calixya Elilai of BKC, who also competed in the open V1 women’s category, said she was initially disappointed when the original event was canceled but was glad the paddlers still had an opportunity to get on the water.

“I was kind of bummed that it was canceled, but I was excited that we would still get to paddle, even if it was just for fun and unofficial,” Elilai said. “The anxiety of actually racing kind of went away, and the new goal became to just have fun and enjoy the experience.”

Elilai said she and her teammates encouraged one another to do their best and enjoy the opportunity, recognizing that the weather was beyond their control.

“We knew there was no controlling the weather, and we had already come to Guam to paddle, so it was about going out there, doing our best and having fun,” she said.

She added that competing in different locations allows paddlers to connect with people from other communities and learn different approaches to the sport.

“Competitions like the Micro Cup help me grow as a paddler because I get to compete in different places, meet other paddlers and learn about how others approach the sport,” Elilai said. “It broadens my understanding of paddling and helps me appreciate the different ways people experience and approach it.”

“We were very pleased with our overall performance. Our paddlers trained hard, both individually and as a team, to prepare for the races in Guam. We competed in six events, earning five gold, one silver and three bronze medals,” expressed Coach Uroi Salii.

AMF also expressed its appreciation to Coach Natalie Calvo and her outrigger canoe club, Poksai, for hosting the knockout races.
